Charts

A chart is the table of its numbers, drawn: columns stacked by day, a sparkline of the last runs, a board of lanes over the next hours. The numbers stay in the page as text, so a screen reader reads the table; a small script only hands them to the stylesheet.

A package of the release

Charts are linked apart, on the screens that show them, beside the two files: css/redz-ui-charts.min.css draws, and js/redz-ui-charts.min.js hands it the numbers - every data-v, data-at, data-from, data-to, data-median and data-now becomes a custom property through the CSSOM, which a strict CSP allows where it would refuse a style attribute. A number is a share, 0 to 1: a value over the largest, a time over the window.

<link rel="stylesheet" href="redz-ui/css/redz-ui-charts.min.css">
<script type="module" src="redz-ui/js/redz-ui-charts.min.js"></script>

Columns by day

table class="chart": its caption is the title, the header cells after the first are the series and draw as the legend, and each row is a day - th scope="row" its label, each td a segment whose data-v is its share of the tallest day, stacked from the first up. The series take eight colors in order, --redz-chart-1 to -8, chosen to stay apart for color-blind eyes in both themes; data-tone gives a segment a state's color instead. A row's title is its tooltip, a row with data-empty a stub on the axis, and a label wrapped in visually-hidden is read, not shown - label every few days when there are many. --redz-chart-height sets the height, 10rem by default; ninety days fit a card 700 px wide.

Shares of a whole

chart-bar with chart draws one row as a single bar across: each segment's data-v is its share of the whole, and the legend under it names the series - write the share in the legend's words too. The bar is 12 px thick; --redz-chart-bar-height sets another.

table class="chart-rows" breaks a whole into rows, longest first: each tr a label, its count and its share in words, and a bar under them whose length is the row's data-v. The header cells name the columns for a screen reader and are hidden from sight. The bars take the first chart color; a row's data-tone gives it a state's.

Sparklines

span class="spark" with role="img" and its summary in aria-label draws one bar per run, oldest first: data-v its share of the longest, data-state - ok, running, timeout, failed - its color, title its tooltip. 18 px high in a row, spark-md 34 px, spark-lg 64 px; spark-median with data-median draws the median as a dashed line.

A board of lanes

table class="lanes" lays a time window across: a header cell lanes-scale holds the captions, each at its data-at, and each row is a lane - its name in the th, which may hold a button, and its track in the td. In a track lanes-tick marks a run at data-at, lanes-tick-once a single one, and lanes-span from data-from to data-to a run that goes on, hatched, with its caption. The red line is now, at the table's data-now - 0, the start, by default; the grid has --redz-lanes-steps parts, 8. Elements that share a data-highlight key light together: a lane, its track included, and its row in a table.

Accessibility

  • A chart is its table: the caption names it, the header cells name the series, each row says its day and its numbers. charts.js gives a chart or chart-rows table the table's roles, since WebKit drops them once CSS lays the table out otherwise.
  • A share bar and breakdown rows say their shares in words - the legend's, the rows' - and the bar repeats them.
  • A color never speaks alone: the legend names every series, a state's segment is named by its header cell, and a sparkline's summary is its name.
  • Three of the light theme's series colors are under 3:1 against the page: the legend and the table are what tell them apart.
  • In forced colors every bar is drawn in the text color; the numbers are in the table.
