A tab that asks before it is left

Before a person's click or key selects a tab, the tablist fires the cancelable redz:select; its detail holds tab, the one asked for, and from, the one being left. A page that must ask first - a form with unsaved edits - calls event.preventDefault() and event.detail.respondWith(promise): the tab is selected when the promise gives true, and otherwise the one being left stays, with the focus. Change the note below, then choose another tab.

  if (form.dataset.changed !== undefined) {

});

Tabs in the address

A tablist with data-hash follows the address: location.hash naming a panel, or anything inside one, selects its tab - when the page loads, and when the hash changes as a person's choice through redz:select, whose refusal puts the address back; a tablist inside a panel too -, and a person's choice writes the panel's id into the address, so a link or a reload opens the same tab. After every selection, the first one too, the tablist fires redz:selected with detail.tab. REDZ.ui.select(tab) chooses a tab from code as a person would - through redz:select, so the page can still refuse - and answers a promise of whether it was selected.

A row that scrolls

A nav or a tab list wider than its box scrolls sideways, and the edge it goes on past fades - data-more says which, start, end or both, as the person scrolls; when the page loads, the current or selected item is scrolled into the row's view, the page itself left where it is.

When the links do not fit

nav-overflow around a nav keeps it on one line: the links that do not fit hide from the end and come back, in their order, as the items of a menu behind a “More” button after the nav. The link the person is on and a link with nav-overflow-keep stay in the row. A link that opens a menu folds with it, as a group of its items under the link's words, and stays in the row while its menu holds the page the person is on; a navbar shows one. A link the page hides with hidden leaves the row and the menu, and comes back when the page shows it. It measures the room its own box has, not the window, so a nav in a narrow column folds as early as it needs to. For a row of links the wrapper is the <nav>, so the menu stays inside the landmark.

  • Links that change the page are a <nav> of links, never tabs: tabs only switch what is already on the page.
  • A link folded into the “More” menu is hidden in the row, so a screen reader and Tab meet it once, in the menu. The button says the page's text more, reports whether its menu is open, and opens it by the menu's keys; a plain click on an item clicks the hidden link itself, so the page's own handlers run.
  • A mark beside a tab's words - a dot, a count - has its own name, so the tab reads as “EN, 1 field outdated”.
