Save bar

A tracked form remembers what it held and marks what changed since; the bar at the foot of the page says what is not saved yet, saves it or discards it, and asks before the changes are left. It sticks to the bottom of the screen while the form is longer than the screen, and a field the keys reach stands above it: while it shows, its height goes to --redz-save-bar-height on the root, which the page's scroll padding at the foot adds.

Examples

data-track on a form tracks it; its value names the form in the bar's words. Change a field - its mark appears, so does a dot on the tab that holds it - then save with the bar or Control S, or discard and undo. Here the page answers in 700 ms and refuses an adult price below the child price; with "A conflict" chosen, it says another person changed the name meanwhile.

The bar's states

At rest the bar says everything is saved; with unsaved changes it names them. The script sets data-state - clean, dirty, saving, saved, error - and a bar written by hand takes save-bar-dirty for the second.

Refused and saved

error is in the danger tone, its words saying how many fields the page refused; saved in the success tone fades back to the bar at rest after two seconds, and under reduced motion it stays until the next change.

2 fields refused: the address is taken, the price is below the child price

The fields that changed

data-changed marks a field whose value differs from the saved one: a bar at the start of a form-control or a form-select - a combobox's face is one - or of a chips field's chip-input box, and a dot after a check or a switch. On a tab it is a dot too. A tracked form sets it; a page may set it by hand.

A form in a dialog

A tracked form in a dialog keeps its own button, which takes Control S when it is the form's only one; the dialog waits while the page saves, and closing it with changes asks first.

What a field is

  • One field per name: a radio group, checkboxes of one name, a select multiple and a combobox's list each count once. A disabled field is compared too, by its value.
  • The saved state is read one task after DOMContentLoaded - after the page's own scripts, so a field a script formats at load is already in it - and one task after a tracked form lands later. A value typed back to the saved one is no change; a field added or removed since is one.
  • data-track="off" on a control or a fieldset leaves it out. Hidden fields are compared but never marked. A field's words in the bar are its data-track-label, else its label, its group's legend or its combobox's label.
  • A value set by code: REDZ.ui.track.refresh(form) draws the change, REDZ.ui.track.baseline(form) takes what the form holds as saved - a dialog refilled for another record.

Saving

Save, Control S, Enter in a field or REDZ.ui.track.save() fires the cancelable redz:save on each changed form in turn, and stops at the first refusal. The page answers with event.detail.respondWith(promise); what the form held when it was sent becomes the saved state once the promise resolves, so what the person typed meanwhile stays changed. Nothing changed: the bar says so, and nothing is sent.

Discarding and leaving

  • Discard puts the saved values back, each with input and change so whatever a field drives follows, fires redz:discard on the form, and offers Undo until the next edit or ten seconds.
  • While anything is changed, a link that leaves the page, a tab whose pane holds a changed form and a dialog's cancel or close ask first: Save and go - the focus starts there -, Discard, Stay. The browser's own question guards the rest, and only while something is changed.
  • An input type="password" with data-secret keeps the stored value while it is empty. A button with command="--clear" for it and data-clear clears it: the field is disabled and emptied, aria-pressed="true", and the form sends the name data-clear gives as 1.

For a page's probes

What a page's own checks may rely on, also in the passport as contracts.track.

The bar's height in pixels stands in --redz-save-bar-height on the root while the bar shows and is gone while it hides; the scroll padding at the foot is that height and the room around the bar, except while the focus is in a save bar. A page that draws its own bar, not a tracked form's, sets and removes it the same way.

Accessibility

  • The bar is a region named by the page's text; its words name the changes, not only their count. They are not a live region: a screen reader hears only that the changes were saved, refused, discarded, or that there is nothing to do.
  • Save and Discard are never disabled; pressed with nothing to do, they say so.
  • It does not print, and it never hides the last field of the form: the form keeps room under it.
  • A changed field's mark is drawn, not read out: the bar's words name the changes; a changed tab is described as not saved. In forced colors the mark is a thicker start border.
