Tokens

The custom properties on :root that the components read: colors by role in pairs that pass WCAG AA in both themes, spacing, type, radii, shadows and motion. A theme changes tokens, never component rules.

How they are made

  • Colors by role: --redz-text, -muted, -faint; --redz-bg-page, --redz-bg, -raised, -hover; a tone, its -subtle fill and its -contrast text for primary, secondary, success, danger, warning and info. --redz-focus and --redz-link are the primary tone unless a theme gives them their own - a white-label palette sets the links' color in --redz-link alone. The product's mark, app-mark, has a pair of its own, --redz-app-mark-bg and --redz-app-mark-fg: the red of the REDZ logo, #cd3428, with white words at 5.1:1 in both themes, or under data-brand the tone and its -contrast words. A project gives its mark another colour by setting the pair on :root in its own stylesheet.
  • Text is at least 4.5:1 on its background, the borders of fields and the focus ring 3:1, in the light and the dark theme - computed in OKLCH and written as checked hex.
  • A component declares a few properties of its own on its root and never on rows or items: hundreds of properties on small elements slow every style recalculation.

A white label from one colour

data-brand on html, or on one region, makes the primary tone of one colour, --redz-brand: the library derives the tone, its -subtle fill, its -contrast text - black or white, whichever reads at 4.5:1 on it, as one of the two always does - the focus ring, the links and the product's mark, in both themes; in the dark one the tone is lightened until it reads on the dark ground. data-brand="#0a7f5a" gives the colour, which the library sets through the CSSOM after its script loads; a page that knows the colour on its server writes --redz-brand in its own stylesheet beside the attribute, and nothing waits for the script. Where the tone is text or a line on the ground - a link, a subtle badge, an outline button, text-primary, an alert's icon, the focus ring - it is --redz-primary-text: in the light theme the colour darkened, its hue kept, until its luminance is at most 0.13, which reads at 4.5:1 on white and on its subtle fill - an engine that refuses a colour's channel as a divisor, Firefox 132 on the floor, darkens it to a CIE lightness of 40 instead; in the dark theme the tone itself. A button keeps the colour as given in its fill - its words identify it. Where the tone fills a state or a value - a checked checkbox, radio or switch, a range, a progress bar, a dot, the current page, step or stat, a row's mark, a picked option's check, a field's changed or invalid mark - it is --redz-primary-fill: in the light theme the colour darkened, its hue kept, until its luminance is at most 0.25, which holds 3:1 on white, on the page's ground and on a track, and keeps its -contrast words at 4.5:1 - Firefox 132 darkens it to a CIE lightness of 56; in the dark theme the tone itself. The other tones follow their own keys the same way: data-brand-secondary, data-brand-success, data-brand-info, data-brand-warning and data-brand-danger - a company's own red for its errors - each derive that tone, its -subtle, -contrast, -text and -fill. Every tone has its --redz-TONE-text and --redz-TONE-fill, the tone itself unless a brand key derives them. The relative colours it is derived with are on the floor: Chrome 119, Firefox 128, Safari 18.

The key may change while the page stands: set on a region after load, data-brand derives the tone there at the next frame, and the region's own data-theme gives it the other theme. A settings screen that lets a person choose the colour asks the library what it will paint: REDZ.ui.brand(colour) answers for both themes with the tone, the words on it, the links, the focus ring, the fill of a checked box or a bar and the ground under them, each #rrggbb, and six contrast ratios, read from the stylesheet the page loads. The words on the tone and the links reach 4.5:1 by themselves, the ring and the fill 3:1; a button keeps the colour as given, so toneOnGround is the one to warn about - a pale colour makes pale buttons. A value that is no colour answers null.
