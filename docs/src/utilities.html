Utilities

Single-purpose classes with Bootstrap 5's names, where the number is the value: p-16, gap-8, fs-14, rounded-8, w-50. Written once from a vocabulary file and committed - nothing is generated where they are used.

Scales

  • Space, in px: 0 2 4 8 12 16 20 24 32 40 48 64 - for m, p and gap with their sides: mt mb ms me mx my, the same for p. ms and me are the start and the end of the line, so they turn right to left.
  • Font size: 11 12 14 16 18 20 24 32 40 48 - 11 for the smallest caption, 40 and 48 for one hero number; line height: 16 20 24 28; radius: 0 4 6 8 12 16 and rounded-full.
  • Width prefixes come on placement and spacing - display, flex, grid, gaps, order, text alignment, margins and paddings: md:d-none, lg:p-24.
  • d-none beats every other display class and component on its element, and a width prefix beats it: d-none md:d-flex shows from md. Page styles outside the layers beat it too: use hidden there.

Aspect ratio

ratio-1x1, ratio-4x3, ratio-16x9 and ratio-21x9 keep an element's shape as its width changes - on a video, a map, an image's frame; ratio-auto lets it go. ratio-1.91x1 is the shape of a link's preview image on social networks, 1200 × 630. The class goes on the element itself: an image inside a frame fills it with w-100 h-100 object-fit-cover.

Sizes in pixels

Where a control or a mark needs a fixed size - a narrow field in a toolbar, a column of codes, a thumbnail - the number is the pixels, as with the spaces:

  • w-56 w-64 w-80 w-96 w-120 w-160 beside the shares w-25 to w-100; h-32 h-40 h-72 beside h-25 to h-100.
  • min-w-56 min-w-120 min-w-160 min-w-200 min-w-240 min-w-288; max-w-80 to max-w-1200, with 360 and 560 among them.
  • size-32 size-48 size-64 size-80 set both sides at once: a square.

Text: kept lines, captions, clamps

text-pre-wrap keeps the lines and the spaces a person typed - a note, an address - and still wraps a long line. tracking-wide spaces the letters of a caption in capitals. line-clamp-2 and line-clamp-3 cut a text after two or three lines with an ellipsis; the whole text stays in the page for a screen reader and for search.

A flash and a sticky panel

flash lights an element's ground once and lets it fade - a row a click elsewhere changed. To light it again, take the class off, let the browser lay the element out, and put the class back. sticky-top keeps an element at the top while the page scrolls: under the app header in the shell, at --redz-sticky-top anywhere else.

Off the screen

offscreen puts an element outside the page, where no one sees it, yet it stays rendered: not display: none, visibility: hidden or the clip of visually-hidden, which bots look for. Two uses:

  • A form's trap for bots: a field a person never fills. Its wrapper carries aria-hidden="true" so no screen reader meets it; the field has tabindex="-1", autocomplete="off" and a plausible name, such as company_url. The server refuses a form that comes back with it filled. Name the class and the field plainly: bots skip what is called a honeypot.
  • A field that takes what a keyboard-wedge reader types - a badge or a fob read at a kiosk - kept focused while nothing else is.

Dividers, rules and icon links

divide-x and divide-y on a parent draw a line between its children, across or down, with no class on any child. vr is a rule between the items of a row. icon-link lines an icon up with the words of a link.
